\section{Application domain and motivation}
% ============================================================

At a fast-food counter or small Imbiss -- like a Burger King register or a
Bratwursthaus kitchen -- staff take an order while ingredients are consumed
behind the scenes. Many small food outlets still track that stock on paper
or in someone's head, which leads to running out of a key ingredient
mid-shift, or over-ordering because nobody had a clear number.

\textbf{CounterStock} is a small database-backed system for a snack bar
counter that ties every sale directly to ingredient stock: each order
automatically deducts what it used, so staff always know what's running low.

Two usage scenarios: a cashier at the register selects menu items and
quantities for a customer; the system checks that enough of every required
ingredient is in stock, records the order, and deducts the ingredients
used. Before closing, a shift lead checks a dashboard of ingredients
running below their reorder threshold and logs a restock delivery, so the
next shift starts with accurate numbers.

\section{Data model -- ER sketch}
% ============================================================

\begin{figure}[ht]
  \centering
  % Swap example-figure.jpg for your own file once you've drawn it.
  \includegraphics[width=0.85\linewidth]{er-sketch.jpg}
  \caption{ER sketch of the snack bar counter data model: entity types, key
  attributes, relationships and cardinalities (at least one N:M).}
  \label{fig:er}
\end{figure}

Figure~\ref{fig:er} shows the ER model of the snack bar counter domain. The
central entity types are \texttt{Staff}, \texttt{MenuItem},
\texttt{Ingredient}, \texttt{Recipe}, \texttt{Orders}, and
\texttt{OrderItem}. \texttt{Staff} has a $1{:}N$ relationship to
\texttt{Orders} (one staff member creates many orders). \texttt{MenuItem}
and \texttt{Ingredient} are connected $N{:}M$ through the associative
entity \texttt{Recipe}, which records how much of each ingredient a menu
item requires. \texttt{Orders} and \texttt{MenuItem} are connected $N{:}M$
through the associative entity \texttt{OrderItem}, which records which
items and how many were part of an order.

% ============================================================
\section{From model to schema}
% ============================================================

The relational schema is derived directly from the ER model above.
Underlined attributes are primary keys; italicised attributes are foreign
keys.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \texttt{staff}(\underline{id}, name, role, email)
  \item \texttt{menu\_items}(\underline{id}, name, category, price, active)
  \item \texttt{ingredients}(\underline{id}, name, unit, stock\_quantity, reorder\_threshold)
  \item \texttt{recipes}(\underline{\emph{menu\_item\_id}}, \underline{\emph{ingredient\_id}}, quantity\_required)
  \item \texttt{orders}(\underline{id}, \emph{staff\_id}, order\_time, status)
  \item \texttt{order\_items}(\underline{\emph{order\_id}}, \underline{\emph{menu\_item\_id}}, quantity, subtotal)
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Normalisation.} Every non-key attribute depends on the whole
primary key and nothing but the key -- e.g.\ in \texttt{order\_items},
\texttt{subtotal} depends on both \texttt{order\_id} and
\texttt{menu\_item\_id} together, not on either alone -- and no column
depends transitively on another non-key column. The schema is in 3NF.

\section{API design}
% ============================================================

At least one reading and one writing endpoint are covered below, with the
JOIN/aggregation and the \texttt{X-API-Key} protection both located.

\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.2}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}p{2.2cm}p{4.6cm}X@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Method} & \textbf{Path} & \textbf{Purpose} \\
\midrule
\texttt{GET}  & \texttt{/menu} & reads menu items with availability derived from ingredient stock vs.\ recipe requirements (JOIN + aggregation: MIN of stock $\div$ quantity required) \\
\texttt{GET}  & \texttt{/ingredients/low-stock} & reads ingredients where stock\_quantity is below reorder\_threshold \\
\texttt{GET}  & \texttt{/orders/\{id\}} & reads order detail with line items and total (JOIN + aggregation: SUM of subtotals) \\
\texttt{POST} & \texttt{/orders} & creates a new order and deducts ingredient stock per recipe (requires X-API-Key) \\
\texttt{POST} & \texttt{/ingredients/\{id\}/restock} & increases an ingredient's stock (requires X-API-Key) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}

\section{Operation with Docker Compose}
% ============================================================

Two services run in \texttt{docker-compose.yml}: \texttt{postgres}, the
database, initialised from a \texttt{.sql} script that creates the six
tables above and seeds a handful of menu items and ingredients; and
\texttt{api}, the FastAPI app, which reads its DB connection string and
the \texttt{X-API-Key} value from a \texttt{.env} file that is not
committed to the repository. \texttt{docker compose up -d} on the lecture
server starts both; the frontend is installed separately via
\texttt{dpkg -i} and points at the server's API URL.

\textbf{Milestones and schedule.}

\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.2}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}p{1.4cm}Xp{1.8cm}@{}}

\textbf{Week} & \textbf{Milestone} & \textbf{Est. hours} \\
\midrule

4--5 & Frontend operates all four screens, packaged as .deb & 12 \\
6   & Tests + documentation + video & 6 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}

\textbf{Risks.} Two near-simultaneous orders could both read the same
ingredient's stock as sufficient before either deduction is written,
over-selling stock that isn't really there -- a check-then-act race
condition. This is mitigated with a database transaction that locks the
ingredient row before checking and deducting stock.

\section{Acceptance criteria and testing}
% ============================================================

\textbf{Acceptance criteria.}
\begin{itemize}[topsep=2pt]
  \item An order that would drive any required ingredient's stock below
        zero is rejected (API returns 409).
  \item \texttt{GET /ingredients/low-stock} returns the correct set of
        ingredients below their reorder threshold (JOIN + filter).
  \item \texttt{GET /orders/\{id\}} returns the correct aggregated total
        (JOIN + aggregation: SUM of subtotals).
  \item Write endpoints without a valid X-API-Key are refused (401).
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Test plan.}
\begin{itemize}[topsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Database:} PK/FK/NOT NULL/CHECK constraints, including
        that stock\_quantity can never go negative.
  \item \textbf{API:} endpoint tests, happy path + error cases, with
        \texttt{pytest} and FastAPI's \texttt{TestClient} against a test
        database (L~08).
  \item \textbf{Business rule:} a dedicated test firing two overlapping
        orders against an ingredient with only enough stock for one,
        verifying the second is rejected and stock never goes negative --
        the test for the race-condition risk named above.
\end{itemize}
